The Life He Left Behind

Before Mireille, there was Sacha. Mireille was his middle name. He took it as his legal first name later.

They met as teenagers and grew up together. They spent their twenties in love, then eloped. His husband knew him long before ballroom, the modifications, and New York, back when Mireille was still figuring out what he wanted.

His husband was one of the first to believe in him. He encouraged his ambitions and saw a star in him early. For a long time they imagined that future together.

Then Mireille moved to New York for fashion and ballroom on a larger scale. His husband stayed behind. They kept the late calls and the occasional visits. Distance made things harder. It did not end them.

In New York he founded House d’Épine and sank into the city’s fashion and nightlife. The glamour suited him, and so did the excess. Through the House he built an auction business in archival fashion, private sales, and pieces with questionable provenance. The schemes got bolder, including the theft of a fantasy bra valued at $50 million. His husband has no part in that work.

They still shared the two cats. The night calls continued, and so did the arguments. Their lives no longer fit together the way they used to. Neither of them had worked out how to let go.

Mireille was becoming someone harder to recognize. Work took more of him. He grew sharper about his appearance and started making decisions alone, including alterations to his body he knew his husband would struggle with.

His husband had always wanted that stardom for him. He had not expected the cost. They began to disagree about New York, the marriage, and how far Mireille was willing to go.

They are still married. Mireille rarely admits how unhappy the distance has made him. He will not give up New York. Losing his husband is still something he never imagined.
